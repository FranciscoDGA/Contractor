**For a whole-house window replacement, $12,000 sits at the top of Fixr's published single-story band, just above the $11,837 typical top Angi reports, and inside HomeGuide's $4,500 to $22,500 band for 10 to 15 windows as of October 2026 — so it is a normal whole-house price only if the quote lists that many windows with a per-window line.**

A single number cannot tell you whether a price is fair, because the same number buys different scopes. Place the quote against published national ranges, then check whether it shows the pieces that move those ranges.

## 1. What published national cost guides say (as of October 2026)

Published guides disagree because they count different window numbers, materials, and project sizes, so read them as bands, not verdicts:

| Published guide | Reported band | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| HomeGuide | $450–$1,500 per window, average $900; $4,500–$22,500 for 10 to 15 windows | Window unit plus installation |
| Fixr | $450–$1,200 per window, average $800; $8,000–$12,000 for a single-story 10- to 15-window home | Size, type, frame and glass materials |
| Angi | $3,436–$11,837 per project, average $7,347; $300 to $2,500 per window | 13,122 verified projects, 2026 |
| This Old House | $517 per window, $7,183 per project | 2026 survey of 1,000 window customers |

> Read the table this way. $12,000 sits at the ceiling of Fixr's single-story whole-house band, a little above Angi's $11,837 top, and inside HomeGuide's range for 10 to 15 windows — so it is a whole-house price, and the quote needs a window count and a per-window line.

Angi publishes single windows at $300 to $2,500, and HomeGuide's national average is $900. These are planning ranges for a national audience, not a price for your house — local labor, count, and material set the figure.

## 2. What makes a quote legitimately higher

Some of the gap between quotes is real scope. Check that each of these lines is named:

- **Window count and size.** Angi prices a medium window of 30 by 36 inches at $300 to $800 and a large window of 48 by 60 inches at $700 to $1,800, so count and size explain most of a gap.
- **Frame material.** Angi's installed prices run $200 to $800 for vinyl, $300 to $1,500 for wood, and $300 to $2,000 for fiberglass. Ask for the material and line on every row.
- **Glass package.** This Old House's 2026 survey reports aluminum frames averaging $453 per window, fiberglass $564, and glass packages reaching $609.
- **Installation method.** A retrofit insert and a full-frame replacement are priced differently, and the quote should say which the number assumes.
- **Removal, disposal, and trim.** Angi notes that old-window removal is often included, but if it is not, expect $50 to $150 per window, with opening resizing at $100 to $500. Replacing all at once often reduces the per-window cost, so ask for the price at your quantity.

None of these are opinions about your windows — they are scope items that should be visible on the page rather than implied.

## 3. Which line items to challenge

Challenge what you cannot decompose:

1. **The single lump sum.** "Window replacement: $12,000" should become a count by style, frame material, glass package, labor per window, removal, and trim.
2. **An unpriced trim or siding allowance.** "Repair as needed" with no amount is a placeholder that arrives later as a change order — see [change orders and why the price goes up](/guides/change-orders-why-the-price-goes-up/).
3. **A missing permit line.** Ask who pulls the permit, who pays the fee, and whether the fee is inside the total.
4. **An unlabeled contingency.** A "miscellaneous" line without a description and a stated rate is an undefined price.
5. **Markup you cannot see.** Materials marked up without a stated percentage are covered in [what is a fair markup on materials](/guides/what-is-a-fair-markup-on-materials/).
6. **A front-loaded payment schedule.** Payments due before meaningful work exists put money at risk early, and down-payment limits vary by state.

The rest of the method — scope first, exclusions next, allowances, then the total — is in [how to read a contractor estimate](/guides/how-to-read-a-contractor-estimate/).

## 4. Get three quotes, then compare scope before totals

One quote gives you a number; three give you a range for your house. The rule of thumb is three written quotes built against the same scope, so the comparison measures price rather than differences in what is included — see [how many contractor quotes should I get](/guides/how-many-contractor-quotes-should-i-get/) and [how to compare contractor bids](/guides/how-to-compare-contractor-bids/).

Run each total through the [quote fairness tool](/tools/quote-fairness/) to see where it sits against published ranges. The same comparison works for roofing — [is $15,000 too much for roof replacement](/guides/is-15000-too-much-for-roof-replacement/) shows the method.

Questions about permits, how a window should be installed, or local code sit outside a price guide — consult your local building authority or a licensed professional.

## 5. Payment and contract terms worth reading before you sign

The Federal Trade Commission's guidance on home improvement scams says to get estimates in writing, read the contract before signing it, fill in every blank space, and never make the final payment until the work is done. [Red flags in a contractor quote](/guides/red-flags-in-a-contractor-quote/) maps that advice onto the document, and [questions to ask before signing a contractor contract](/guides/questions-to-ask-before-signing-a-contractor-contract/) has the wording worth requesting in writing. A quote with a deadline attached asks you to skip competing estimates and a contract read at your own pace — do not sign yet.

## The bottom line

$12,000 is inside published national bands for whole-house window replacement as of October 2026, so the number alone does not settle it. What settles it is whether the quote lists window count, frame material, glass package, removal, and trim — and whether two more quotes on the same scope land in the same neighborhood.
