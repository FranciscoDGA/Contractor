**For a full kitchen remodel, $45,000 sits at the top of Fixr's published national band and just above the typical ranges Angi and HomeAdvisor report as of October 2026, while remaining inside HomeGuide's $15,000 to $50,000 full-remodel band — so the number is defensible only if the quote is buying a full remodel with new cabinets and appliances rather than a refresh.**

A single number cannot tell you whether a price is fair, because the same number buys different scopes. Place the quote against published national ranges, then check whether it shows the pieces that move those ranges.

## 1. What published national cost guides say (as of October 2026)

Published guides disagree because they measure different scopes, kitchen sizes, and material tiers, so read them as bands, not verdicts:

| Published guide | Reported band | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| HomeGuide | $15,000–$50,000 at $150 to $250 per square foot | Full kitchen renovation, structural layout unchanged |
| Fixr | $15,000–$45,000, average about $27,000 | Kitchen remodel by material quality and project scope |
| Angi | $14,589–$41,559, average $26,945 | 13,100 verified projects, 2026 |
| HomeAdvisor | $14,588–$41,555, average $26,942 | $75 to $250 per square foot |

> Read the table this way. $45,000 sits at the ceiling of Fixr's band, a little above the upper figures Angi and HomeAdvisor publish, and inside HomeGuide's full-remodel band — so the quote has to be buying a full remodel, cabinets and appliances included, to belong there.

Angi and HomeAdvisor publish near-identical figures, so treat those two rows as one measurement rather than two. These are planning ranges for a national audience, not a price for your kitchen — local labor, kitchen size, and finish level set the actual figure.

## 2. What makes a quote legitimately higher

Some of the gap between quotes is real scope. Check that each of these lines is named:

- **Cabinets.** HomeGuide prices stock or semi-custom cabinetry at $4,500 to $15,000 installed and custom at $15,000 to $30,000, the widest spread in a kitchen quote. Ask for the line and construction, not the phrase "new cabinets."
- **Countertops.** HomeGuide puts countertops at $1,500 to $6,000 for an average kitchen, or $50 to $150 per square foot installed.
- **Appliances.** HomeGuide prices a matching package of dishwasher, microwave, range, and refrigerator at $2,500 to $6,800 installed.
- **Flooring and backsplash.** HomeGuide lists flooring at $300 to $3,000 and backsplash installation at $500 to $1,700.
- **Plumbing and electrical.** HomeGuide lists this at $1,200 to $5,000 or more — the line that separates a refresh from a remodel in every published band.
- **Demolition and permits.** HomeGuide puts demolition at $500 to $2,500 for a gut. Ask who pulls the permit, who pays the fee, and whether it is inside the total.

None of these are opinions about your kitchen — they are scope items that should be visible on the page rather than implied.

## 3. Which line items to challenge

Challenge what you cannot decompose:

1. **The single lump sum.** "Kitchen remodel: $45,000" should become cabinets, countertops, appliances, flooring, plumbing, electrical, demolition, and permits.
2. **An unpriced cabinet or counter allowance.** "Allowance as selected" with no amount is a placeholder that arrives later as a change order — see [change orders and why the price goes up](/guides/change-orders-why-the-price-goes-up/).
3. **A missing permit line.** Ask who pulls the permit, who pays the fee, and whether the fee is inside the total.
4. **An unlabeled contingency.** A "miscellaneous" line without a description and a stated rate is an undefined price.
5. **Markup you cannot see.** Materials marked up without a stated percentage are covered in [what is a fair markup on materials](/guides/what-is-a-fair-markup-on-materials/).
6. **A front-loaded payment schedule.** Payments due before meaningful work exists put money at risk early, and down-payment limits vary by state.

The rest of the method — scope first, exclusions next, allowances, then the total — is in [how to read a contractor estimate](/guides/how-to-read-a-contractor-estimate/).

## 4. Get three quotes, then compare scope before totals

One quote gives you a number; three give you a range for your kitchen. The standard rule of thumb is three written quotes built against the same scope, so the comparison measures price rather than differences in what is included — see [how many contractor quotes should I get](/guides/how-many-contractor-quotes-should-i-get/) and [how to compare contractor bids](/guides/how-to-compare-contractor-bids/).

Run each of the three totals through the [quote fairness tool](/tools/quote-fairness/) to see where it sits against published ranges. The same band comparison works for bathrooms — [is $25,000 too much for a bathroom remodel](/guides/is-25000-too-much-for-a-bathroom-remodel/) applies it there.

Questions about whether a layout change is permitted, how work should be installed, or what local code requires sit outside a price guide — consult your local building authority or a licensed professional.

## 5. Payment and contract terms worth reading before you sign

The Federal Trade Commission's guidance on home improvement scams says to get estimates in writing, read the contract before signing it, fill in every blank space, and never make the final payment until the work is done and you are satisfied. [Red flags in a contractor quote](/guides/red-flags-in-a-contractor-quote/) maps that advice onto the document itself. A quote with a deadline attached is asking you to skip competing estimates and a contract read at your own pace — do not sign yet, and use [questions to ask before signing a contractor contract](/guides/questions-to-ask-before-signing-a-contractor-contract/) for the wording worth requesting in writing.

## The bottom line

$45,000 is at the top of the published national bands for a kitchen remodel as of October 2026, so the number alone does not settle the question. What settles it is whether the quote breaks out cabinets, countertops, appliances, plumbing, electrical, demolition, and permits — and whether two more quotes written against the same scope land in the same neighborhood.
